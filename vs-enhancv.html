Design vs ATS-safe formatting

Here is the tension: many of Enhancv's most attractive templates use columns, sidebars, and graphics, the exact elements that can confuse an ATS parser. They look great to a human and can score worse with the software that screens you first. ATSHack deliberately outputs a plain, single-column layout with standard headers, because that is what parses cleanly. If a recruiter will read your resume directly through a referral, Enhancv's design can help; if it goes through an ATS, plain wins.

Pricing: per-resume vs monthly

Enhancv runs about $19.99/month, less if you commit semi-annually, after a trial. ATSHack is $1 per tailored resume with no subscription on the no-account path. For a short, focused job search, paying per resume is cheaper than a monthly builder subscription.

Tailoring per posting

Enhancv's free ATS checker flags issues, but you still edit the resume yourself in its builder. ATSHack rewrites your existing resume to match each specific posting and returns the file, which is the part that actually moves your match score.
