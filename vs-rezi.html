ATSHack vs Rezi.

Rezi is a popular AI resume builder with ATS optimization baked in, priced around $29/month or a one-time $149 lifetime license. It is built to create a resume from scratch inside its editor. ATSHack does the opposite job: it takes the resume you already have, rewrites it to match a specific job posting, and hands you a finished PDF and DOCX for $1, no account.

Build from scratch vs tailor what you have

Rezi shines if you are starting with nothing and want a guided editor to assemble a resume section by section. But most job seekers already have a resume; they just need it matched to each posting. Paying a monthly subscription to a builder is overkill for that. ATSHack assumes you have a resume and focuses entirely on tailoring it to the job you are applying to right now.

Pricing: per-resume vs subscription or lifetime

Rezi's Pro plan runs about $29/month, with a one-time $149 lifetime option that is genuinely good value for heavy long-term users. ATSHack is $1 per tailored resume with no account on that path, plus an optional $15/month Pro tier if you want a saved dashboard. If you apply to a handful of jobs over a few weeks, $1 each beats any subscription. Prices can change, so check each site.

The no-fabrication rule

ATSHack only uses content from the resume you upload. It rephrases and reorders, it never invents jobs, dates, or skills. If you let any AI builder generate content from scratch, review every line, because invented experience is a fast way to fail a reference check.
